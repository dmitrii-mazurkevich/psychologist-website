About me

Enough to decide whether you want to write to me, without a wall of credentials.

I have been practising for nine years. Before that I did something else entirely, which I mention because a fair number of the people I see are in the middle of exactly that kind of change and are convinced it makes them a failure.

How I work

My training is in cognitive behavioural therapy, and I went on to schema therapy. In plain terms: we start from what is happening now and what you would like to be different, and where the same thing keeps returning, we look at where the pattern was learned. Usually it was a reasonable response to something, once.

Practically, sessions are conversations. I ask a lot of questions. Sometimes we agree on something to try between sessions, and if you do not do it, that is information rather than a failure.

What I do not do

  • I do not diagnose. That is a psychiatrist's job, and if I think you should see one, I will say so plainly.
  • I do not prescribe medication and I will not comment on medication another specialist has prescribed you.
  • I do not work with acute crises or active addiction — these need a different setting, and pretending otherwise wastes your time.
  • I do not promise timelines. Anyone who promises you a number of sessions before meeting you is guessing.

Confidentiality, concretely

Nothing you say leaves the room. Not to your partner, not to your parents, not to whoever is paying for the sessions. If a family member writes to me about you, I will not confirm that you are a client.

There are two exceptions and I state them at the first session rather than burying them here: a direct, immediate threat to your life or someone else's, and a lawful court order. In supervision — which every practising therapist is required to attend — cases are discussed with everything identifying removed.

Notes are minimal, kept encrypted, and you can ask to see them.

Training and supervision

  • MSc in clinical psychology, 2015
  • Cognitive behavioural therapy, four-year certified programme
  • Schema therapy, advanced level
  • Regular individual and group supervision, ongoing
  • Member of a national psychotherapy association, bound by its ethical code

On a real site every line here would name the institution and the year, and link to a verifiable registry entry. Vague credentials are the most common warning sign in this field.

Languages

I work in English and Russian. People often find it easier to talk about difficult things in a language that is not their first — and just as often, the opposite. Either is fine, and you can switch mid-sentence.
