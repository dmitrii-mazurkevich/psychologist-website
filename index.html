What people come with

You don't need a good enough reason

Most people spend a long time deciding whether their problem is serious enough. It nearly always is. These are the things I hear most often.

  • Anxiety that has stopped being occasional
  • A loss, or a change that turned out to be a loss
  • Burnout, and the guilt that comes with it
  • The same argument in every relationship
  • Never feeling good enough, whatever you do
  • Difficulty saying no, and exhaustion from that
  • Moving country, and everything that came with it
  • No particular reason — just wanting to understand yourself better
Six questions

Is this for me?

This is not a test and it does not diagnose anything. It takes about a minute and nothing is sent anywhere — the answers stay in your browser and disappear when you close the page.

I put off things that actually matter to me
It is hard to explain to people close to me what is going on
I notice myself ending up in the same situation again and again
I wake up already tired
I try not to be left alone with my own thoughts
It feels like I am coping worse than I should be

Six questions cannot assess anybody, and this one does not try to. It only reflects back what you have just said. A real assessment happens in conversation, over time.

How it works

What actually happens

Most of the fear before a first session is about not knowing what will happen. So here it is, in order.

You write to me

A couple of sentences is enough — you do not have to formulate the problem properly. I answer within one working day and offer two or three times.

The first consultation, 50 minutes

You tell me what is going on, in whatever order it comes out. I ask questions. By the end we both have a sense of whether this will work.

We decide together — or you don't

If it fits, we agree how often to meet. If it does not, I say so and, where I can, suggest a colleague who suits you better. That happens and it is fine.

Regular sessions

Usually weekly at the same time. Some people come for six sessions, some for two years. We review where we are every couple of months, out loud.

About me

Nine years, mostly listening

I trained in cognitive behavioural therapy and went on to schema therapy, which is the part that deals with the patterns people describe as "I know exactly what I am doing wrong and I do it anyway".

I work with adults. I am in supervision, as every practising therapist should be, and everything said in the room stays there.

More about how I work

If it feels unbearable right now

Therapy is not emergency care and I cannot answer urgently. If you are thinking about harming yourself, please use one of these now, without waiting for an appointment. You do not have to explain yourself to them.

  • 112 — the emergency number across Europe and Georgia. Ask for medical or psychological help.
  • 24/7 crisis line — put your verified local number here. This demo deliberately does not print one, because a wrong number in this block is worse than none.
  • Someone nearby — a friend, a relative, a neighbour. Not being alone for the next few hours matters more than who exactly is with you.
